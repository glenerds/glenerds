How your data works

Storage: everything is saved in this browser's local storage on this device only. Nothing is sent anywhere and there is no account.

Reminders: a single web file cannot send push notifications. HouseplantLog shows what's due when you open it — it will never pretend to remind you in the background.

Backup: download a JSON backup to keep your collection safe — restoring it on any device brings everything back.

Sample data: the flask button snapshots your real data, shows a made-up example, and restores your snapshot exactly when tapped again.

Care basics

Water

Most houseplants do best when the top inch or two of soil (about a finger's depth) is dry before watering again. Water thoroughly until it drains, then empty the saucer — roots sitting in standing water rot. Dry-loving plants like cacti and snake plants want the soil to dry out much further between drinks.

Light

Direct sun means hours of sun hitting the leaves. Bright indirect is a bright room a step back from the window. Medium is a few feet from a window. Low is a dim corner — most plants merely survive there. Leggy, stretched growth usually means a plant wants more light; scorched or bleached patches mean too much, too fast.

Feeding

Fertilize mainly while a plant is actively growing (spring and summer for most). Follow the strength printed on your fertilizer's label — double strength doesn't feed twice as well and can burn roots. Most houseplants need little or no food in winter.

Misting

Misting raises humidity around leaves for a short while — it doesn't replace watering. It suits many tropical plants, but let leaves dry afterwards; foliage that stays wet invites spots and mildew. Good everyday airflow helps.

Repotting

Repot when roots circle the bottom of the pot, push out of the drainage holes, or water runs straight through. Go one size up — roughly 2 inches (5 cm) wider — with fresh potting mix. A much bigger pot holds more water than the roots can use and can keep soil soggy.

Your intervals, your call

Plants vary a lot — even two of the same species differ with pot, soil, and the light in your home. HouseplantLog doesn't promise any schedule fits a species. You set every plant's intervals, and the app counts from what you actually did. If a plant looks thirsty before its date, water it and the schedule learns from your tap.

What this app doesn't do

HouseplantLog doesn't identify plants from photos and can't diagnose pests or diseases — no single file honestly can. It's a schedule and a logbook: yours, on your device, with nothing behind a paywall.
