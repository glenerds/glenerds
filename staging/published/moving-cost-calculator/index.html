What actually drives moving costs

Three things dominate the price: how much stuff you have (home size), how far it goes (distance), and how much labor you buy (DIY vs. full-service). Everything else — packing, storage, valuation — is a meaningful but smaller adder. The single most expensive mistake is comparing quotes that aren’t for the same service: a cheap full-service quote next to a container quote is not an apples-to-apples comparison. Get every quote in writing with the same inventory list, and be wary of any estimate that comes in far below the others — that is the classic setup for a hostage-load scam where the price doubles on moving day.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is this estimate?

It is a ballpark built from national averages — good for budgeting and for spotting a quote that looks wrong, not a substitute for real quotes from movers.

Why is the range so wide?

Season, exact route, stairs, long carries, and how precisely you estimated your inventory all move the final number. The low–high range reflects that real-world spread.

Is DIY always cheaper?

Usually, but not always by as much as people expect once you add truck rental, fuel, packing materials, and your own time. Run both scenarios above before deciding.

What is valuation coverage?

The mover’s liability for damage. Basic coverage is minimal by law; full valuation costs more but actually covers replacement value. Worth pricing for a long-distance move.
