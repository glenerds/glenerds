🎯 THE PROBLEM
Your landlord kept part of your security deposit and handed you an itemized deduction list. Some charges might be fair — but carpet with 6 years of wear, a fee for normal move-out dust, paint that's already past its useful life? Landlords bank on renters not doing the math.

✅ THE FIX
DepositCheck audits every deduction line by line. Enter your deposit and each charge, and it applies useful-life depreciation math (that 6-year-old carpet on an 8-year life means you owe at most 25% of replacement cost), totals your fair share versus what was withheld, and flags every overcharge — then builds a printable dispute letter pre-filled with your numbers.

⚠️ READ FIRST
Your audits are saved in your own browser only — nothing is uploaded anywhere. One HTML file; your data never leaves your device. No account, no subscription — pay once, use it for every move.

🚀 AFTER DOWNLOAD
1. Open depositcheck.html in any browser — no install, no account.
2. Enter your deposit, what was withheld, and what was returned.
3. Add each deduction from your landlord's itemized list.
4. Read your results, print the report, and generate your dispute letter.

✨ WHAT IT DOES
- Line-item audit with useful-life depreciation tables: carpet, paint, appliances, blinds, fixtures, and more
- Wear-vs-damage classification: normal wear and tear is the landlord's cost, not yours
- Results dashboard: fair share, overcharged total, and % of your deposit at stake
- Printable audit report with a per-item breakdown
- Dispute letter builder, pre-filled with your line-item results — download, print, or copy
- Move-out legal checklist so you don't miss a deadline or a step
- Saved audits with JSON export/import — keep a record of every dispute
- Customizable useful-life values with one-click reset to defaults
- Full dark mode, sample data to explore, and optional sound effects

🛠️ HONEST NOTES
Guidance only — not legal advice. Deposit rules, deadlines, and remedies vary by state; check with a tenant-rights organization or attorney for your situation. The depreciation math follows HUD-style useful-life conventions, but every case is different.
